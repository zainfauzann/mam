Our story

One association,
every discipline.

The Motorsports Association of Malaysia (Persatuan Sukan Bermotor Malaysia) was incorporated on 28 February 2007 and is the national governing body for motorsport. We are recognised by the Ministry of Youth and Sports (KBS), and we are the only Malaysian body recognised by the FIA for four-wheel motorsport and by the FIM for two-wheel motorsport.

From club hill climbs to international championships at Sepang, MAM sanctions and regulates circuit racing, rally, karting, drag, motocross, time attack, drift, hill climb, autocross, autoslalom, vintage and 4×4 events.

"We exist so that every Malaysian who wants to race, at any level, can do it safely and fairly, with results recognised worldwide."

MAM issues competition licences to more than 1,200 drivers and riders, sanctions over 60 events a year and works with more than 80 affiliate clubs across all 13 states and the federal territories.

Heritage

Our history

1963

The National Expansion

Following the formation of the Federation of Malaysia, the primary automotive body (originally the Automobile Association of Malaya) is rechartered to cover Sabah and Sarawak, giving the country one national framework for road rules and racing.

1985

International recognition & Shah Alam

Shah Alam Circuit (Batu Tiga) opens and brings international racing to Malaysia. Over the next years the country moves from local club racing to hosting the World Sportscar Championship and later the Malaysian Motorcycle Grand Prix.

1999

The Sepang revolution

Sepang International Circuit (SIC) opens and Formula 1 arrives. Events at this level need a specialist sporting authority, and technical and sporting management moves to FIA and FIM standards.

2007

Formal incorporation of MAM

The Motorsports Association of Malaysia is incorporated on 28 February 2007 to run the sporting side of motorsport, separate from the general automobile associations.

2019

Transition of authority

The FIA and FIM transfer National Sporting Authority (ASN) and National Federation (FMN) status from the Automobile Association of Malaysia (AAM) to MAM. MAM becomes the only body that sanctions races and issues licences in Malaysia.

2026

Today's roadmap

MAM has more than 1,200 licence holders and 80 affiliated clubs under one code. The National Karting framework moves young drivers towards international racing, and the renewed FIM cycle runs from 2026 to 2030.
